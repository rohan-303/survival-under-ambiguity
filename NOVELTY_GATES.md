# Novelty and Sufficiency Gates

## Gate N — Novelty

**Question:** What exact theorem, algorithmic capability, or validated scientific finding remains after placing the closest papers beside the manuscript?

The contribution must not be reducible to a routine combination of known contamination neighborhoods, censoring observation operators, robust inverse problems, censored-data partial identification, DRO, or OT methods. The gate requires a source-backed comparison and theorem-level audit.

Statuses: **OPEN**, **CONDITIONAL**, **PASS**, **FAIL**. Status after Step 01: **OPEN**.

## Gate S — Sufficiency

**Question:** Even if the proposed result is correct and genuinely new, does it materially change how a survival analyst understands, diagnoses, or reports uncertainty?

Evidence should show a meaningful frontier, impossibility phenomenon, assumption-sensitive tightening, overconfidence diagnosis, stable-decision procedure, or ambiguity-exposing algorithm. A mathematically new but scientifically trivial inequality is insufficient.

Statuses: **OPEN**, **CONDITIONAL**, **PASS**, **FAIL**. Status after Step 01: **OPEN**.

Neither gate is evaluated by this bootstrap. Passing tests only establishes repository scaffolding, not scientific validity.
