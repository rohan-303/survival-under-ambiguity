## Selected leader

None. The search found three credible independent candidates, but each still has a material prior-art or data-access gate. Selecting a leader now would manufacture confidence the evidence does not support.

## Why no leader was selected

- Competing-risk cause allocation has the clearest clinical decision consequence, but the exact distinction from competing-risk transport and foundation-model evaluation is not yet verified.
- Multi-state transition reliability has unusually clear evidence of practical need, but `calibmsm` is a direct and strong overlap threat.
- Observation-process shortcuts are important and independent in principle, but realistic public longitudinal data and a valid non-censoring intervention remain unresolved.

## Strongest novelty threats

1. **Competing risks:** existing cause-specific models, robust survival methods, and new competing-risk foundation models may already contain the proposed decomposition.
2. **Multi-state:** `calibmsm` may already cover the substantive calibration contribution; only external transport and decision reversal could distinguish it.
3. **Observation process:** the idea may collapse into missingness, irregular sampling, or censoring-shift work unless the observation-policy estimand is made genuinely distinct.

## Strongest sufficiency concern

A calibration or reliability plot is not sufficient by itself. A candidate must change a model choice, clinical threshold, deferral/escalation decision, data-collection policy, or external-validation conclusion. A small benchmark gain is not enough.

## Exact next research question

Before implementation, independently verify whether a competing-risk model can preserve acceptable all-cause calibration while materially misallocating cause-specific risk and reversing a pre-specified clinical action under a real site/temporal shift, in public data with reproducible competing-risk fields.

This is a research question for a future dedicated Step 00, not an authorization to implement now.

## What must be verified before implementation

1. Complete primary-source search for the exact all-cause-versus-cause-specific transport/decomposition claim.
2. Official access and field audit for at least two datasets, preferably SEER plus a credentialed EHR source.
3. A written estimand with cause, horizon, threshold, action, and transport split fixed before outcome inspection.
4. Classical and recent baseline code availability, versions, and exact endpoint compatibility.
5. A synthetic counterexample only as a theory sanity check, never as the sole empirical evidence.
6. A kill test showing existing calibration or external-validation metrics do not already detect the failure.
7. Explicit separation from all Steps 01–08: no contamination ambiguity, audit certification, RMST identification, or re-censoring novelty.

## Decision rule

Remain at `NO_CLEAR_LEADER` unless one candidate passes the above checks. Do not create a new repository or implementation directory from this closeout.
