# Rare-Group Fragility Theory

Search/derivation date: 2026-09-18. This document separates models that were previously being called “rare-group contamination.” No statement is labeled `NOVEL`.

## Scope frozen for Step 03

Primary theory: population recoverability of subgroup survival under right censoring, global contamination, subgroup prevalence, subgroup-specific follow-up, and label assumptions. Secondary theory: finite multi-bin survival/RMST operators. Out of scope: OT as primary geometry, neural generation, real data, high-dimensional covariates, informative censoring, finite-sample confidence regions, and causal effects.

## Model definitions

### G-A: trusted label, fixed clean prevalence

The clean joint law has `P(G=1)=pi`; observed `G` is trusted; the clean prevalence is fixed; contamination replaces arbitrary whole recorded rows with global mass `epsilon` and may allocate that mass to any observed subgroup. The target is the clean conditional event/survival probability in `G=1`.

### G-B1: whole-row replacement with untrusted subgroup label

`R=(1-epsilon)P+epsilon Q` on `(G,Y,Delta)`, but clean `pi=P(G=1)` is not fixed and the contaminant can choose both label and outcome. For observed rare-label prevalence `r`, exact Huber coordinate geometry gives

`max(0,(r-epsilon)/(1-epsilon)) <= pi <= min(1,r/(1-epsilon))`.

If `r` is not fixed, clean prevalence has no nontrivial universal interval. A clean conditional target `S_1` also requires `pi>0` and a definition of latent membership. There is no single sharp `S_1` formula without fixing the observed joint law, prevalence restrictions, and the outcome/label coupling. **OPEN.**

### G-B2: label-only corruption

The clean outcome is retained but the observed label is generated by a declared corruption channel. Under known symmetric binary flip rate `eta<1/2`, a binary observed prevalence `r` identifies `pi=(r-eta)/(1-2eta)` before truncation/feasibility checks. Under arbitrary label replacement or unknown flip rate, prevalence is only partially identified and can be broadly uninformative. Conditional survival still requires the label-channel and the joint relation between clean `G` and outcomes; no universal formula is claimed.

### G-C: marginal-preserving contamination

The contaminant is constrained to have the same subgroup marginal `Q(G)=P(G)=(pi,1-pi)`. Conditional on each group, the contamination fraction is the same global `epsilon`; the global adversary cannot concentrate more mixture mass into the rare group. The one-bin conditional diameter is therefore

`min(1, epsilon/[(1-epsilon)g_1])`,

with no `1/pi` factor. This is a different assumption, not a correction to G-A.

### G-D: per-group contamination cap

Let conditional group contamination rates satisfy `eta_g <= bar_epsilon_g` and global mass satisfy `sum_g pi_g eta_g <= epsilon`. For a single group, the largest permitted conditional rate is

`eta_g^max = min(bar_epsilon_g, epsilon/pi_g)`.

The one-bin diameter is

`min(1, eta_g^max/[(1-eta_g^max)g_g])`,

with the obvious convention that a rate at least one gives diameter one. This model represents external group-specific audit information.

## G-A derivation

Changing only the rare conditional event probability from `p_1` to `p'_1`, while keeping the majority law and clean prevalence fixed, changes the clean recorded joint law by

`TV(P^rec,P'^rec)=pi*g_1*|p_1-p'_1|`.

Equal-global-epsilon Huber neighborhoods overlap exactly when this TV distance is at most `epsilon/(1-epsilon)`. Hence

`R_GA(epsilon,pi,g_1) = min(1, epsilon/[(1-epsilon)pi*g_1])`.

This is an **ELEMENTARY_DERIVATION** from Step 02’s finite-alphabet Huber overlap lemma, not a new theorem claim.

Edge cases and frontier:

- `epsilon=0`, `pi>0`, `g_1>0`: diameter 0.
- `pi=0` or `g_1=0`: diameter 1; the conditional target is undefined at `pi=0`, so the limit is an uninformative boundary, not a valid subgroup estimand.
- Away from saturation, `dR/depsilon = 1/[(1-epsilon)^2 pi g_1] > 0`.
- Away from saturation, `dR/dpi = -epsilon/[(1-epsilon)pi^2 g_1] < 0`.
- Away from saturation, `dR/dg_1 = -epsilon/[(1-epsilon)pi g_1^2] < 0`.
- Saturation occurs exactly when `epsilon >= pi*g_1/(1+pi*g_1)`.

Equivalent critical surfaces:

`epsilon_crit(pi,g)=pi*g/(1+pi*g)`;

`pi_crit(epsilon,g)=epsilon/[(1-epsilon)g]` for `g>0`, with complete nonidentification when `pi <= pi_crit`;

`g_crit(epsilon,pi)=epsilon/[(1-epsilon)pi]` for `pi>0`, with complete nonidentification when `g <= g_crit`.

These derivatives describe the toy modulus and do not establish practical finite-sample behavior.

## Majority/minority asymmetry

For a group with prevalence `pi_g` and follow-up `g_g`, the same G-A formula is `R_g=min(1,epsilon/[(1-epsilon)pi_g g_g])`. Thus the rare group is worse only through the smaller effective observable mass `pi_g g_g`; a majority group with poor follow-up can be more fragile than a rare group with complete follow-up. “Rare groups are worse” is not a theorem without comparing both effective masses.

## Multi-group allocation

For trusted labels and fixed prevalences, changing only group `j` by conditional amount `d_j` costs `pi_j g_j d_j` in clean recorded-law TV in the one-bin model. Under pairwise Huber overlap, the resource constraint is

`sum_j pi_j g_j d_j <= epsilon/(1-epsilon)`, `0<=d_j<=1`.

For the worst single-group diameter, the adversary selects the smallest `pi_j g_j`. For a weighted aggregate objective, the finite problem is a fractional knapsack/LP; no universal greedy rule is claimed beyond the linear one-bin model.

## Multi-bin survival

With subgroup-specific censoring operators `A_{q,g}`, the clean recorded joint operator is block-structured, with subgroup block mass `pi_g A_{q,g}`. For a subgroup functional `w_g^T p_g`, exact fixed-observed-law bounds remain LPs. The one-bin scalar `g_g` cannot be substituted pointwise without checking the full operator: different survival horizons and RMST weights use different columns and censoring cells. The correct general object is an operator-specific modulus, with the scalar `pi_g g_g` appearing only in the one-event reduction.

## Literature kill-test

Targeted search found survival subgroup analysis under censored heterogeneous AFT models, but it addresses subgroup identification/estimation rather than exact global-contamination allocation.[10] Work on uncertainty in latent subgroup membership shows that imputed subgroup labels can materially affect subgroup inference, but it is causal subgroup sensitivity rather than this survival/Hüber population geometry.[11] These are relevant threats, not evidence of novelty.

The strongest current threat remains that G-A is a direct corollary of general Huber geometry. The strongest remaining candidate is a theorem comparing G-A, G-C, G-D, and G-B under a common observed-data contract and showing when audit information changes the recoverability frontier. That candidate remains **POTENTIALLY_NOVEL**, not `NOVEL`.

The Step 02 literature threats include classical competing-risk bounds and robust OT under Huber contamination.[1][2][3]
Survival DRO, conditional OT partial identification, and generative ambiguity sets constrain broad framing claims.[4][5][6]
Dependent-censoring bounds, survival sensitivity analysis, and the unresolved DropCens package lineage remain additional threats or access gaps.[7][8][9]
